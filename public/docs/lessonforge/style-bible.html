Style Bible: The Glow in the Shed

The rules every shot in this lesson is made to, whoever or whatever makes it. The locked prompt blocks in lesson.toml are the machine-readable version of this page. If the two ever disagree, fix lesson.toml. Where a delivered shot breaks a rule, the rule says so.

1. Look

Rule
Format 16:9, 1920x1080, 24 fps delivery
Capture feel 35mm film, spherical lenses, soft grain, gentle halation
Lenses 24 / 28 mm for wides, 35 / 50 mm for mediums, 85 / 100 mm for close-ups and inserts
Depth of field Shallow on everything closer than a medium shot
Palette Umber, brass, slate blue, ochre. Nothing saturated except the lamp flame
Contrast Low-key. Let shadows go to near black; never lift them to "see everything"

2. Light logic

The shed has one light source per state, and every shot is written to obey it.

State Source Direction Colour
Lamp lit (S02-S05, S10) Brass oil lamp on the table Camera left, low Warm, about 2000 K
Lamp down (S06, S07, S21) The vials themselves From below and from the shelves Faint pale blue, very dim
Courtyard day (S16, S17) Overcast sky Top, soft Neutral grey
Exterior night (S01) Lamp through the glass roof From the shed outward Warm amber against cool blue

The delivered cut breaks this table in three places. In the lamp-lit shots (S02-S05, S10) the glass roof is lit like overcast daylight, a second, cool source from above. In S06 the roof brightens into daylight and green leaves after the lamp goes down. And the vials in S06 and S07 do not visibly glow; only S21's do, and they read blue-green.

The presenter set copies the lamp-lit state: warm key from camera left, a practical brass lamp in the background. That is what lets AI plates and live-action plates cut together.

The delivered presenter shots break the first half of that: the approved A still came out keyed from camera right, opposite the lamp, and setup B and all seven presenter shots follow it. A and B match each other, so the cuts between them hold.

3. Characters

Marie Curie, 34, in 1902. Slender, oval face, high forehead, deep-set grey eyes, frizzy ash-blond hair in a loose low bun. Plain dark navy wool dress, high collar, long sleeves, no jewellery, no apron, no gloves. Bare hands with slightly reddened, cracked fingertips: radium burns were already marking her hands by this period.

Pierre Curie, 43. Appears once (S16), in the background, soft focus, back or profile only. Limiting him to one soft shot keeps the second identity out of the consistency budget. The delivered S16 breaks this: Krea-2 rendered him sharp, working at a barrel just behind her, his face in three-quarter view, in all three candidates. He appears once, so there is nothing to match.

Locking method used on this lesson:

  1. Identical description text in every prompt (pulled from lesson.toml, never retyped).
  2. Video shots start from an approved keyframe (image-to-video), never from text alone.
  3. A producer picks each keyframe by eye.

Planned, not run on this lesson:

  • A character LoRA for Marie (trigger mcurie1902), trained from the approved keyframes (README, Next).
  • QC gate: ArcFace similarity to a reference sheet (refs/marie_turnaround.png, not made yet), threshold 0.45, once the face model is installed. On this lesson the face check skipped every take.

What the delivered cut shows: Marie reads 15 to 20 years older than 34 (S02, S03, S05, S07, S16); in S06 her hair is sleek and platinum, not frizzy; and in the S04 and S10 inserts her clothes read light slate-blue, not dark navy.

4. World

  • The shed: bare wooden walls, tar-black floor, pitched glass roof that leaks, worn pine tables, black cast-iron stove. No blackboard in the prompt: Curie's account puts one in the shed, but in every test render it filled with garbled chalk writing, so it was left out of the set description. Krea-2 still added one, with faint chalk marks, in S02 and S05 (delivered). Glassware: flasks, white porcelain evaporating dishes, small stoppered vials.
  • The courtyard: cobblestones, plain grey stone buildings, iron cauldron on a brick fire pit, canvas sacks of brown ore dust mixed with pine needles.
  • Period: Paris, 1902. No electric bulbs, no wristwatches, no plastic, no modern lab safety gear. In the delivered S02 a dark cable runs from the oil lamp's base (S03 shows a stub of it), which makes the lamp read as electric.

5. Accuracy rules (the "no hallucinations" list)

Rule Why
The radium glow is faint pale blue, never neon green Curie described "feebly luminous" vials like faint fairy lights. Radium's own glow is blue; luminous paint glowed because of its zinc-sulfide phosphor, not radium's own light. The one glow in the delivered cut (S21) reads blue-green
No smoke or vapour coming off the vials Models add it by default for "radioactive"; it is not real
No text, numbers or readable dials in any generated frame Generated text is the most common hallucination. Every readable word and number in the master is added in motion graphics. Exception in the delivered cut: faint, unreadable chalk marks on a blackboard in S02 and S05
No atomic nucleus imagery in the 1898-1902 scenes The nucleus was discovered in 1911. Modern explanations appear only in the graphic labelled Modern understanding (S19)
No protective gear The Curies worked bare-handed; showing gloves would be false comfort
Dates on screen only from the fact-check table in README.md One source of truth for every number

6. Negative prompt strategy

On this lesson no negative prompt is evaluated: Krea-2 turbo, Qwen-Image-Edit with the Lightning LoRA and H3 turbo run at CFG 1, and the production graphs have no LF:negative node, so accuracy comes from the positive prompt, the set and selection (models doc, CFG 1: docs/models_and_samplers.md). The layers below apply to engines that run real CFG, such as the Wan 2.2 alternate.

Negatives are layered, so each shot carries only what it needs:

  1. Global (style.negative): period breakers, text and numerals, the green-glow myth, anatomy failures.
  2. Video (style.video_negative): motion failures such as morphing, identity drift and flicker. Wan uses negatives; Krea-2 turbo at CFG 1 ignores them, so keyframe accuracy comes from the positive prompt and selection.
  3. Per shot (negative_extra in the shot list): the specific thing that shot tends to invent, such as faces in hands-only inserts or readable dials on the electrometer.

7. Cutting rules

  • Cut on action: S05 cuts as the flame drops, not after.
  • 180° line in the shed: the lamp is camera left. Written as Marie facing camera right; in the delivered cut Krea-2 turned her toward the lamp, so she faces camera left in S02, S03 and S05, and in S07 her eyes go to the vials at camera right. The lamp never changes sides.
  • S06 is a silent beat with no VO. Hold at least 4 seconds and let the glow land. The delivered S06 holds 4 seconds but shows no glow (§2).
  • Graphics never sit on top of a face.
